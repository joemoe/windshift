Why did my boat go round in a full circle without touching the sails?

Trimmed sails only "know" the angle between the boat and the wind. If you never touch them and keep the boat sailing nicely, you hold that angle – so your heading follows the wind. If the wind direction itself changes along your track, you turn with it.

On calm nights and mornings the shores cool much faster than the lake. Cold air drains off the Pfänder, out of the Bregenzerwald and the Rhine Valley, down from the Appenzell hills and off the Bavarian/Württemberg shore – from every side towards the warmer open water, where it converges. Near such a convergence the wind blows from the north on the north side, from the south on the south side, from the east on the east side. Holding the same angle to that wind (e.g. a beam reach) carries you tangentially around the convergence zone – one full turn of the compass and roughly back to where you started, with the sails never touched.

Try it: load “Calm night – the circle”, then drop a boat 1–3 km from the calm spot in the middle of the eastern lake. Föhn, Pfänderwind or a westerly push that calm spot around or wipe it out.
